5. Prove canonical workspace ownership.

6. Prove one workspace lease/fencing authority.

Audit all:

- recovery leases
- control-plane workspace leases
- supervisor locks
- monitor locks
- in-memory locks

For actual execution against a workspace:
FlowDeck durable lease + fencing epoch must be authoritative.

Control-plane locking may coordinate metadata, but it must NOT independently grant mutation authority.

Test:
- two concurrent control tasks targeting same workspace
- Heidi mutator and control-plane mutator collision
- retry after lease transfer
- stale supervisor after fencing epoch changes
- process restart during lease ownership

A stale execution owner must never publish successful evidence.

- duplicate control-plane submission cannot duplicate FlowDeck mutation

Add explicit mapping tests.

- parent/control task cannot become SUCCEEDED
- manual review/reconciliation remains available where required

9. Prove recovery convergence.

Simulate full process restart at key lifecycle points:

A.
Request accepted, no child started

B.
Child started, no side effect yet

C.
Mutation side effect may have occurred, response lost

D.
Mutation completed, verification not persisted

E.
Verification persisted, parent completion not persisted

F.
Parent FlowDeck success persisted, control projection not updated

G.
Control state says success but FlowDeck remains UNKNOWN

On restart:
- reload durable FlowDeck truth
- reacquire leases/fencing
- do not blindly rerun successful work
- do not blindly retry UNKNOWN mutation
- reconcile operation-specific state
- repair control projection from FlowDeck
- do not trust stale in-memory supervisor state
